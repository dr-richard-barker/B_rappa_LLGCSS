Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our original research article, **"Beyond Classification: Machine Learning Latent Decoders Reveal Conserved Cosmic Radiation Kinetics, an Antioxidant Shield Deficit, and Fixative Fidelity in *Brassica rapa* (Lunar LEAF)"**, for consideration in *npj Microgravity*.

As human spaceflight enters the Artemis era, establishing bioregenerative life support systems (BLSS) on the lunar surface requires identifying crops capable of surviving chronic galactic cosmic radiation (GCR). Here, we investigate the 3-way factorial RNA-seq study of *Brassica rapa* (Wisconsin Fast Plants) exposed to 40 cGy GCR simulation at Brookhaven National Laboratory under the NASA Artemis III Lunar LEAF candidate investigation.

Confronting the central question—*"Is spaceflight stress response merely a machine learning classification exercise?"*—we demonstrate that while automated classification tools (DeepSpace Stress-Recognition System and 41D CVAE ROS Decoders) rapidly triage spaceflight stress and infer acute post-exposure kinetics (3.84 h nadir), machine learning latent projections uncover fundamental, actionable biological insights that classification alone misses:

1. **The "Antioxidant Shield Deficit":** We demonstrate that anthocyanins act as a vital chemical buffer against ionizing radiation. Wild-Type plants buffer radiation ionization into a secondary hydrogen peroxide cascade (51.1% probability), whereas the *anthocyaninless* (*anl*) mutant suffers an immediate, unbuffered superoxide free-radical burst (24.9% probability, peaking at 2.23 h) and constitutive baseline resilience depression (RRI = 0.638).
2. **Preservative Fixative Parity (DRS vs. RL):** We provide the first cross-transcriptome verification of NASA's Deep Space Preservative (DRS) against laboratory-standard RNAlater (RL), confirming high correlation (Pearson r = 0.297, P < 10⁻¹⁵) and RRI delta trajectory parity within 0.03 units. This certifies ambient dry-storage stabilization hardware for autonomous lunar return missions.
3. **Core DNA Damage Repair Induction:** Wild-Type plants successfully mobilize core homologous recombination and base-excision repair loci (BRCA1 +2.03 log₂FC, PARP2 +0.86, RAD51 +0.16) along with the upstream flavonoid gateway (PAL1 +0.61, CHS +0.77, F3H +1.51), whereas *anl* mutants exhibit significant negative interaction terms across these protective loci.

All datasets derive from the NASA Open Science Data Repository (OSDR), and all code, figures, and interactive web tools are openly accessible in CoSE style.

We believe this work will be of strong interest to the broad microgravity, astrobotany, and space radiation biology communities.
