As humans plan sustained agriculture beyond Earth, a practical question is which plant traits are
altered by chronic galactic cosmic radiation (GCR). Floral scent — which underlies pollination and
is one of the most biochemically well-mapped outputs of plant specialised metabolism — is a natural
trait to examine, yet its radiation response has not been tested. Our manuscript builds a reusable
framework for the question in *Brassica rapa* (Wisconsin Fast Plant) and reports a deliberately
careful first answer.

We highlight three contributions:

1. **A reproducible analytical framework.** We reconcile two independent RNA-seq datasets — a
   floral-scent contrast and a 0/40 cGy radiation dose-response — curate and validate a tiered,
   363-gene floral-volatile gene set, and test radiation perturbation with a correlation-aware,
   power-characterised gene-set method. All code and derived tables are openly available and
   regenerate with a single script.

2. **An honest, statistically bounded result.** After correlation-aware and multiple-testing
   correction we find no significant effect of 40 cGy on the scent gene set. Because a power
   analysis shows the test resolves coordinated shifts as small as ~3–4%, this is an *informative*
   null that bounds any coordinated radiation response — not a failure to detect. We believe such
   rigorously bounded negative results are valuable and under-reported in space-biology
   transcriptomics.

3. **A specific, falsifiable prediction.** The only sub-threshold trend, and independent
   comparative-genomic evidence, converge on the volatile-tailoring methyltransferase/acyltransferase
   family — the enzymes that define scent across *Petunia*, snapdragon and rose. We predict that any
   radiation effect on scent acts on these tailoring enzymes rather than the conserved core
   synthases, a hypothesis directly testable in higher-powered data.
